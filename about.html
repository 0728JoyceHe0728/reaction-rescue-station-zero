The story

Nine hours from home,
everything goes dark.

Helios-9 is an orbital chemistry station returning a crew of six to Earth when a meteor strike tears through its oxygen loop.

Mira Chen, the station’s chemical engineer, and KEPLER, its emergency-control AI, discover that five backup reactors can still generate the water, clean air, coolant, and sealant the crew needs.

The player becomes Mira, adjusting reaction coefficients to conserve every atom. With each balanced equation, another system returns online. The final reaction restarts the main generator before the rescue window closes.

Game description

Stabilize five reactors

Players adjust coefficients in five progressively harder chemical equations. A live inventory counts atoms on each side of the reaction, while precise feedback identifies elements that do not yet balance. Cooperative onboarding, increasing complexity, and a complete rescue storyline turn abstract notation into a goal-driven puzzle.

Learning objectives

After playing, learners can:

  1. 1

    Balance chemical equations

    Use coefficients to make the number of atoms of each element equal on the reactant and product sides.

  2. 2

    Apply conservation of mass

    Explain why atoms are rearranged but not created or destroyed during a chemical reaction.

  3. 3

    Distinguish coefficients and subscripts

    Correctly calculate atom totals and use the smallest whole-number ratio to express a balanced equation.

Learning design

Built for productive struggle

Live evidence

An atom ledger updates with every coefficient change, making the conservation rule visible.

Targeted feedback

Mistakes identify the first unbalanced element and its two atom totals, rather than only marking an answer wrong.

Progressive challenge

Reactions move from two reactants to four species, including compounds with multiple elements.
